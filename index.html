Privacy policy.

Last updated: November 24, 2026 · Übermensch for iPhone (iOS 27)
EnglishDeutschFrançaisEspañolItaliano

Übermensch is a self-tracking app for iPhone. It keeps what you choose to record or import (body data, food, money, tasks, journal entries, daily check-ins) and looks for links between them. Everything you record is stored on your iPhone. There is no Übermensch account, no Übermensch server and no analytics, so the developer never receives your records.

This page lists every case in which the app uses the network, what leaves the phone in each case, and how to delete everything.

In short

  • Your records live on your iPhone, and in your own iPhone backups and the copies you make yourself.
  • AI runs on the device by default. The optional Apple Private Cloud Compute stays off until you turn it on.
  • The app goes online only for what you turn on or ask for: to download an AI model you chose, to send AI requests to Private Cloud Compute if you turned it on, and to look up, if you turned that on, a barcode the built-in database doesn’t know. Apart from that, it asks the App Store for Pro’s prices when it starts, and iOS may download Apple’s speech model the first time you dictate. Purchases go through the App Store.
  • No accounts, no ads, no tracking, no analytics or crash-reporting SDKs. We do not sell or share data, because we do not have any.

What we collect

Nothing, through the app. We run no server, so there is nowhere for your records to go. No sign-in, no email collection, no telemetry, no advertising identifier. The only personal data we can ever receive is what you send us yourself by email (see Contacting us).

What stays on your iPhone

  • Journal entries, daily check-ins, mood, body sensations, identity statements, reflections and notes
  • Tasks, skills and practices
  • Food and drink entries with calories and nutrients, and exercise
  • Expenses, income, budgets and recurring payments
  • Body measurements and lab results you enter or import
  • Cycle entries, if you use cycle tracking
  • Photos you attach to journal entries, and moments you pick from Apple’s Journaling Suggestions
  • Imported books and highlights
  • Your conversations with the AI, its notes (“AI memory”), observations and hypotheses
  • Supplements, life events, and day length, if you turned it on
  • Your own events and their marks, decisions with how they turned out, and letters to your future self
  • Settings

iOS encrypts this data at rest with its standard Data Protection. The app does not sync. Its data is included in your iPhone backup (iCloud Backup or a computer backup) if you make one. That backup belongs to your Apple Account and we cannot access it. Downloaded AI models are excluded from backups. The app keeps up to three weekly safety copies of its own database on the device, so a damaged database can be recovered. Delete all data removes them too.

Automatic clean-up. Chat transcripts older than 365 days are deleted. Observations and suggestions you dismissed are deleted 60 days after you dismiss them. The hypothesis record itself is kept.

System search. Journal entries, the AI’s observations, the built-in instructions and the ones you add, your recent meals, expenses and supplements, and your lab panels are added to the on-device Spotlight index so you can find them in iPhone search. The index stays on the device and is cleared when you delete all data. You can hide the app’s content from search in iOS Settings → Apps → Übermensch.

Camera, microphone and photos. Barcodes, receipts, meal photos, menus, supplement labels, screens of measuring devices, banking-app screenshots and photos of lab reports are read on the device, and camera images never leave it. Dictation uses on-device speech recognition: the audio is discarded and only the transcript is saved. The first time you dictate in a language, iOS may download Apple’s speech model for it; that download comes from Apple, and none of your audio or text is sent with it.

Notifications are local reminders scheduled on the device, and only if you turn them on.

Location (optional). Under Daylight in Privacy & Data, “Use my location once” asks for your position and keeps only its latitude, rounded to about a kilometer, on the device to compute how long the days are where you live. Nothing is tracked. “Forget my location” removes the saved latitude.

Calendar (optional). If you turn on “Calendar load” in Privacy & Data → Context, the app counts the hours booked in your calendar on the device. Events are read, never stored or written.

Air pressure (optional). If you turn on “Air pressure from this iPhone” in Privacy & Data → Context, the app reads the pressure sensor of the iPhone when it opens, at most every three hours, and keeps the readings on the device. iOS asks for Motion & Fitness access for this; the app reads nothing else through it: no steps, no activity, no altitude.

Import from another journal. An export from Daylio, Day One or Apple Journal that you pick is read on the device. After you confirm the preview, its entries and moods become part of your record; the file itself is not kept and is not sent anywhere.

Reading aloud. Reviews and chat replies are read aloud with the voices built into iOS, on the device.

App lock. Face ID, Touch ID and the passcode are checked by iOS. The app only learns whether the check passed; it never sees biometric data.

Folders you choose. The weekly copy and the Markdown mirror write files into a folder you pick in Files. The weekly copy is a full copy of the app’s data, including values imported from Health; the Markdown mirror holds journal entries only. If you pick a folder with a cloud provider, the files are under that provider’s terms, not ours; we never see them. A bank statement you import is read once: its rows are saved as expenses and income, and the file itself is not kept.

Apple Health (HealthKit)

Health access is optional, and you choose each category in Apple’s permission sheet. After you grant access, the app imports up to a year of your Health history and refreshes it when you open the app.

Read

Steps; workouts; resting heart rate, heart-rate variability, blood pressure, respiratory rate, VO₂ max, blood glucose, sleeping wrist temperature; walking heart rate average and heart-rate recovery after a workout; blood oxygen and breathing disturbances during sleep; active energy and exercise minutes; environmental sound levels; time in daylight; the total scores of Health's anxiety (GAD-7) and depression (PHQ-9) questionnaires, never the individual answers; weight, body-fat percentage, waist circumference; sleep, including sleep stages; mindful minutes; state of mind, with the feelings and what it was about that you named in Health; the number of alcoholic drinks. With cycle tracking (requested only for a female profile or when you log a period): menstrual flow, menopausal state and bleeding after menopause. Lab results from Health Records, only when you import them, where Apple offers Health Records. Heart rate during a practice, only when you switch the pulse on for that practice: the Apple Watch app then runs a workout session for the length of the practice so the watch measures the heart continuously, and that session is not saved to Health as a workout.

Written back (only values you enter in the app)

Weight, blood pressure and resting heart rate; fiber, sugar, sodium, caffeine and alcoholic drinks from meals you log; your daily mood as a state-of-mind entry, and, with cycle tracking, menstrual flow. When you delete a body measurement, a meal or a period day in the app, the copy the app wrote to Health is removed too.

Rules

  • Health data is never sold, never shared with third parties, and never used for advertising, marketing or data mining.
  • The app never sends Health data to us or to any server of its own and does not sync it anywhere. Like the rest of the app’s data, values imported from Health are part of your iPhone backup if you make one, and of the exports and copies you make yourself: the Markdown, JSON and data-file exports and the weekly copy to a folder you pick.
  • Apart from copies you make yourself and what you ask Siri or Shortcuts to read (the “Weekly Sleep” shortcut hands them your last seven nights of sleep, under Apple’s privacy policy), Health-derived values can leave the phone in only one way: the optional Private Cloud Compute. If you turn it on, an AI request it answers may include values such as sleep, resting heart rate, mood or cycle phase (see below).
  • Readings are shown as “signals”, not diagnoses. Übermensch is not a medical device and does not give medical advice. Cycle estimates are not contraception and not a fertility test.

AI

You choose the AI in Profile → AI. “Auto” uses the first one that is ready.

  • Apple Intelligence (Apple Foundation Models). Apple’s model, built into iOS on iPhones that support Apple Intelligence. It runs on the device and the app downloads nothing.
  • Übermensch AI. The app’s own model, a fine-tune of Liquid AI’s open LFM2.5 2.6B, offered on iPhones with 8 GB of memory next to the stock LFM2.5 2.6B it was trained from. When you tap Download model, the model you chose is fetched once from Hugging Face (about 2.9 GB). After that it runs entirely on the device.
  • Private Cloud Compute (Apple). Optional and off by default. Once you turn it on, it answers in three cases: when you select it; under Auto, when no on-device model is ready; and under Auto, for the monthly review, where it is tried first. Each time it answers, the request is sent to Apple’s Private Cloud Compute servers. The request is your question, or a background task such as a review, a reply to a journal entry or sorting a receipt into a category, plus the records the app adds as context, which can include journal text, check-ins, mood, spending, food and Health-derived values. Apple states that this data is used only to answer the request, is not stored, and is not accessible to Apple. We never receive it. You can turn Private Cloud Compute off at any time in Profile → AI.

The AI may also consult a reference library stored inside the app: public-domain handbooks on practical skills and first aid, and notes on the same topics written in our own words. Searching it happens on the device. AI answers are general information, not medical advice.

Purchases

Übermensch Pro is sold through the App Store by the month, by the year (the yearly plan starts with two free weeks while your Apple Account is eligible) or once, for good. A subscription renews until you cancel it in your Apple Account settings; Profile → Übermensch Pro → Manage subscription opens them. Apple processes the payment under Apple’s privacy policy. The app checks your purchase or subscription on the device with Apple’s StoreKit, and we receive no payment details. To get Pro on a new iPhone, sign in with the same Apple Account and tap Restore purchases in the app.

What we do not do

  • No advertising and no ad SDKs.
  • No tracking: no advertising identifier, no fingerprinting, no data brokers.
  • No analytics or crash-reporting SDKs (no Firebase, Mixpanel, Amplitude, Sentry or similar).
  • No social-media SDKs, no location tracking (a rounded position for day length is the only use of location, and only when you turn it on), no contacts.
  • No selling or sharing of personal data.

Your controls

  • See what is stored: Profile → Privacy & Data.
  • Edit or delete a single record: from the record itself.
  • Export: Profile → Privacy & Data → Export, as Markdown, JSON or a full data file; Übermensch Pro adds a printable year sheet (PDF). Exports leave the app only when you share them.
  • Move to a new iPhone: export the data file, then on the new phone go to Profile → Privacy & Data → Restore → Import data file. A normal iPhone transfer or backup restore also brings the data along.
  • Delete everything: Profile → Privacy & Data → Delete all data. It erases every record, journal photos, the books and instructions you added, the app’s safety copies of its database and its Spotlight entries. Downloaded AI models stay; remove them in Profile → AI. Deleting the app also removes all of its data from the device. Exports and backups you made stay under your control.
  • Health access: iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Health → Übermensch. Values the app wrote to Health stay in Health until you delete them there.
  • Private Cloud Compute: Profile → AI. Online barcode lookup: Profile → Privacy & Data → Barcodes. Notifications: iOS Settings → Notifications → Übermensch.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR, the UK GDPR or California law), you have the right to access, correct and delete your personal data and to object to its processing. Your app data never reaches us, so we hold none of it, and you exercise these rights directly in the app with the controls above. If you want an email you sent us deleted, ask and we will delete it. You can also complain to your local data-protection authority. We do not sell or share personal information.

Consumer health data

We do not collect, share or sell consumer health data. Health information you record is processed by the app on your device. If you turn on Private Cloud Compute, Apple processes the relevant part of it only to answer the request.

Children

Übermensch is intended for adults and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect personal data from anyone, because the app has no server to collect it on.
