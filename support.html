Private Cloud Compute is Apple’s optional cloud model. It is off by default; you turn it on in Profile → AI, where Apple offers it. When it answers, the request (your question, or a task the app runs on its own, such as a review) and the records it needs go to Apple’s servers. The privacy policy has the details.

Without any AI, everything else still works: recording, Ledger, Apple Health import, the statistics in Pulse → Observations, and export. AI screens tell you what to turn on.

Which one to pick? Apple Intelligence needs no download and is the right default where it speaks your language. Übermensch AI, our fine-tune of Liquid AI’s LFM2.5 2.6B, is the choice where Apple Intelligence is turned off or not offered, or does not speak your language. The stock LFM2.5 2.6B it was trained from is offered next to it. Download over Wi-Fi, with at least 3 GB of storage free.

I picked a model, but chat doesn’t answer. Open Profile → AI and check that the model shows as downloaded. If it doesn’t, finish the download or switch to Auto.

Moving to a new iPhone

Option 1: a normal iPhone transfer. Quick Start, iCloud Backup or a computer backup restores Übermensch’s data together with the rest of the phone. AI models are not transferred; download yours again in Profile → AI.

Option 2: a data file (no iCloud needed).

  1. On the old iPhone: Profile → Privacy & Data → Export → Data file (full copy). One .aar archive holds the database and everything in Documents (journal photos, books, instructions). Save it to Files or AirDrop it.
  2. On the new iPhone: install Übermensch, then Profile → Privacy & Data → Restore → Import data file, and pick that archive.
  3. Fully close the app (swipe it away) and open it again.

Option 3: the weekly copy. In Privacy & Data → Copy to a folder, pick a folder in Files. Once a week the same archive is written there and the newest three are kept. The archive holds all of the app’s data, including values imported from Apple Health, and stays wherever you chose to put it.

The database already on the new phone is set aside, not deleted. Markdown and JSON exports are for reading and archiving; only the data file can be imported back. Apple Health data moves with Apple Health itself.

Deleting data

  • One record: edit or delete it from the record itself.
  • Everything: Profile → Privacy & Data → Delete all data. Records, journal photos, the books and instructions you added and the app’s safety copies of its database are all erased; downloaded AI models stay. This can’t be undone; the app returns to the first-run setup.
  • Values the app wrote to Apple Health: in the Health app, tap your profile picture → Apps → Übermensch, then delete its data there.
  • Deleting the app removes all of its data from the iPhone. Exports and backups you made stay until you delete them.

Übermensch Pro

Übermensch Pro is tied to your Apple Account and comes three ways: by the month, by the year (the yearly plan starts with two free weeks while your Apple Account is eligible) or once, for good. It adds every hypothesis after the first one with the evidence behind it, weekly and monthly reviews, n-of-1 experiments (tests you run on yourself), and two printable pages: a doctor sheet and a year sheet. Recording, the timeline, Apple Health, Practice, the AI dialogue and export stay free. Weekly and monthly reviews need an AI model the iPhone can run, so on an iPhone that can run none they are not offered.

Restoring a purchase. On a new iPhone or after reinstalling, sign in to the App Store with the same Apple Account, then open Profile → Übermensch Pro and tap Restore purchases. Apple handles refunds at reportaproblem.apple.com.

Canceling a subscription. Profile → Übermensch Pro → Manage subscription, or your Apple Account settings. Cancel at least 24 hours before the current period ends; Pro stays on until the period ends.

Other questions

Does it work offline? Yes. Recording, barcode lookup, food and exercise search and dictation work without a connection (the first time you dictate in a language, iOS may download Apple’s speech model for it). The internet is needed only to download an AI model, for Private Cloud Compute if you turn it on, and for App Store purchases. Barcodes come from a database built into the app; looking up unknown codes online is a separate switch in Profile → Privacy & Data → Barcodes, off by default.

Is it a medical app? No. Übermensch is not a medical device and does not diagnose. Readings are shown as signals, and cycle estimates are not contraception and not a fertility test. For anything that worries you, see a doctor.

Where is my data? On your iPhone. See the privacy policy.
